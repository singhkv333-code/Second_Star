§0

Executive summary

View Markets is conceptually right — Belief → Expression → Deployment is a genuinely differentiated wedge, and the engine underneath (event studies with BMP significance, deflated Sharpe, MinTRL, two-dial confidence, tiered builders, honest-short resolution) is more rigorous than anything an Indian retail product ships. The problem is that none of that rigor reaches the screen, and what does reach the screen actively undermines it. The showcase leads with a cherry-picked "best past run," dresses strategy tiers in Polymarket's Yes/No costume without Polymarket's one honest number (price = probability), buries the position calculator at the top of the wrong page, and — worst — ships a data pack in which every strategy's rigor block is a copy-pasted artifact from an unrelated monsoon/rural study.

Four failures, in order of severity:

FailureOne-line statementClass
1 · Data integrityThe pack's scores blocks, confidence evidence, and disclosure copy are a rural-basket research artifact repeated 20×; the three copies of best_expression disagree with each other; transmission_count: 2 with an empty transmission array. This violates the never-fabricate boundary today.P0
2 · Strategy edgeTiers are templates stamped uniformly on every view (all 8 aggressive tiers are the same ATM/+5% bull call spread); evidence windows are calendar windows, not event-conditioned abnormal returns; nothing is graded against what's already priced in. The suspicion of "no edge" is structurally correct.P0
3 · Information architectureTen stacked sections with no narrative arc, duplicate charts on two pages, three different hero numbers for one strategy, calculator three clicks deep on the evidence page instead of inside the deploy decision.P1

The fix is equally structured: Part II encodes a research-backed doctrine — five axioms, an event taxonomy that decides which evidence standard each view type is allowed to claim, a formal Edge Grade (five gates, multiplicative, mapped onto the existing verdict ceilings in confidence.py), and an honest projection/sizing method (shrinkage → cost haircut → band, fractional Kelly). Part III redesigns the three surfaces around the prediction-market patterns worth stealing — one dominant number per card, resolution rules as a trust object, progressive disclosure — while explicitly rejecting the betting affordances. Parts IV–V sequence the work so data honesty lands before any restyling.

North-star sentence for the revamp. A user should be able to say: "I believe X. Pivot showed me what the market already prices, what happened the last N times, how sure I can honestly be, three ways to express it at my risk level, and what deploying ₹50,000 would realistically look like — net of costs, as a range, not a promise."

1.2 · Strategy edge — why the strategies feel like they have none

The construction pipeline (expressions/dispatch.py → 21-archetype catalog → tier knobs → builders) is good scaffolding: honest instrument resolution, purity/liquidity screens, β-hedged pair sizing, refusal envelopes. But as shipped, the event never conditions the strategy:

  • One trade, eight costumes. All 8 aggressive tiers are the same bullish ATM/+5% vertical spread (net premium in a 2.06–2.35% band) regardless of whether the view is "gold rises" (→ a Titan spread!?) or "Nifty 30k". Direction, moneyness, and structure never respond to the view's implied move, IV state, or event date.
  • No surprise dimension. The literature is unambiguous — returns follow surprise vs consensus, not outcomes (Bernanke–Kuttner: an expected cut moves the index ~zero). The full expectations pipeline exists (expectations.compute_surprise, implied_move.py, PM adapters) and zero rows are populated; nothing on any surface answers "what's already priced in?" — the single most defensible differentiator in the spec.
  • Evidence isn't abnormal returns. event_study.py implements market-model abnormal returns with BMP + sign tests — and nothing on the serving path calls it. The pack's per-occurrence returns are raw windows, so every "positive in N of M" conflates the strategy with beta.
  • Statistically weak claims presented as track record. "Positive in 20 of 31" is a one-sided sign-test p ≈ 0.075 — indistinguishable from chance at conventional thresholds, before any multiple-testing deflation. The card's hero number, "best past run +64.8%," is the sample maximum: the one statistic guaranteed to be inflated by selection.
  • Rigor exists but is disconnected. PSR/DSR/MinTRL/num_trials, CAAR significance, clean-factor orthogonalization, exit variants — all computed by the v3 battery, all present in schema, none real in the pack and none rendered. Four disjoint score systems (construction alignment, v3 battery, _trust_from_distribution's 78/60/42 table, deployment trust block) with no combiner.
  • Costs invisible. Every envelope carries costs.round_trip_bps; no surface renders it. At Indian retail scale (~0.25–0.35% round trip delivery equity; several % of premium on single-stock options) costs halve small event edges — hiding them overstates every number shown.

1.3 · Information architecture — correct pieces, no story

  • No narrative arc. The detail page stacks: stance → chart → pills → description → table → six-card grid → similar. The natural investor question sequence — what's the claim → what does the market think → what's the evidence → how do I express it → what would it cost me — appears nowhere in that order.
  • Three hero numbers for one strategy. Card: "best past run +64.8%". Deep dive: "typical +11.0% median". Stats grid: "+9.8% mean per occurrence". Same strategy, three surfaces, three contradictory headline framings (one of them fed by the desynced best_expression).
  • Duplicated modules. The Monte-Carlo chart renders on both detail (BenchmarkComparison) and deep dive; per-occurrence returns render as a list (detail) and as bars (deep dive); the deep-dive "honest stats" repeat the calculator's outputs on the same page.
  • The calculator is in the wrong place (StrategyDeepDive.tsx:182): a sizing widget mounted as section 1 of the evidence page, reachable only via gallery → detail → expand row → "See the full analysis" — while the actual deploy decision (StrategiesTable row → Deploy) has no sizing step at all. The number a user deploys with never touches the calculator.
  • Dead-end deploy. On /view-pack, onOpenWorkflowById is a no-op and pack ids don't exist in the DB — Deploy POSTs fail silently. Follow hearts silently revert. The page demos a promise the page itself breaks.
  • No URLs. Detail and deep dive are body-swaps with no route change — nothing is linkable, back-button behavior is trapped, and the deep dive can't be landed on.

§2 · Part II

The Strategy Doctrine — how event-based strategies are built and graded from now on

This is the encoded philosophy requested: a set of rules concrete enough that a strategy either passes or it doesn't, grounded in the event-study, backtest-inflation, and market-microstructure literature (full citations in the Appendix), and mapped onto machinery Pivot already has so it is an assembly exercise, not a rewrite. It should be lifted nearly verbatim into Markdowns/STRATEGY_DOCTRINE.md and referenced from CLAUDE.md when implementation begins.

2.1 · Five axioms

AxiomStatementResearch anchor
A1 · Surprise, not outcomeMarkets price the consensus; returns follow actual − expected. A view's tradeable content is the gap between what's priced and what the user believes — a correctly-predicted outcome that was 85% priced pays almost nothing. Every Event/Relative view must carry an Expected vs Your-view vs Gap frame; strategies are graded on the gap.Kuttner 2001; Bernanke & Kuttner 2005 (expected component moves S&P ~0; surprise 25bp ≈ 1%)
A2 · Abnormal, not rawAll historical evidence is market-model abnormal returns (α̂, β̂ from an estimation window; AR = R − expected R), never raw windows. Raw windows launder beta into "edge." Calendar windows are only legal for Theme views and must be labeled as rolling windows, never as occurrences.MacKinlay 1997 (JEL); BMP standardized tests
A3 · Small N is structuralIndian macro events give N ≈ 8–31 analogs. At that size the normal approximation fails and one outlier owns the mean, so: nonparametric tests (sign / Corrado rank), verdict ceilings, no point forecasts, and no claim stronger than "suggestive." 20-of-31 positive is p ≈ 0.075 — the doctrine's canonical example of a number that must be displayed with its weakness.MacKinlay 1997 small-sample regime; Bernard & Thomas 1989 (41-of-48 quarters is what real robustness looks like)
A4 · Deflate before displayAny number a user sees has already survived: trial-count deflation (DSR logic — with 20 tried variants, a best Sharpe ≈ 1 is noise), a ≥50% decay shrinkage on projections, and Indian round-trip costs. If deflation kills the edge, the death is shown (honest no_edge), never hidden.Bailey & López de Prado 2014 (DSR); Harvey-Liu-Zhu 2016 (t > 3); McLean & Pontiff 2016 (−26% OOS, −58% post-publication)
A5 · Two risks, two dialsBeing right about the event and long the wrong instrument is a losing trade. Outcome confidence (will the belief resolve true?) and expression confidence (does this instrument capture it?) are graded separately and never averaged — already the law in confidence.py::two_dial_score; the doctrine makes it a display law too.Dubinsky-Johannes 2019 (event variance ≠ direction); Pivot confidence.py

2.3 · The Edge Grade — one score, five gates, existing ceilings

Today four disjoint score systems coexist with no combiner (construction alignment; the v3 battery's outcome/expression dials; _trust_from_distribution's 78/60/42 table; the deployment trust block). The Edge Grade replaces the display layer of all four with one pipeline, computed per expression, stored in config.scores.edge_grade, rendered everywhere the same way.

Kalshi's number is honest because it's a settled claim on $1; Pivot's grade is honest because it's the deflated residual of a documented pipeline.

2.4 · Projection & sizing — what replaces "best past run"

The projection system converts historical evidence into the only forward statement the doctrine permits: a cost-adjusted, shrunk range. This also fixes the three-hero-numbers problem — there is exactly one canonical statistic per strategy (the shrunk net median) and one canonical picture (the band).

# per expression, computed at pack-generation time
w        = N / (N + 30)                      # shrinkage toward zero; N=30 → w=0.5,
                                             # matching the McLean–Pontiff ≥50% decay base rate
p50_net  = w · median(AR_occurrences) − round_trip_costs
band     = [w·P25 − costs,  w·P75 − costs]   # empirical quartiles, same shrinkage
tail     = MC p05 (block bootstrap, already computed) — displayed as "worst 5% of re-runs"
sizing   = quarter-Kelly on the shrunk edge, capped at 5% of stated capital per view
           f* = w·(p̂ − (1−p̂)/b) / 4        # p̂ = shrunk hit rate, b = |P75/P25| asymmetry
display  = "Typically +4.6% net over ~3 months (range +1% to +9%, worst 5% ≈ −16%)"
forbidden = best_episode_pct as a headline · any un-shrunk mean · any pre-cost number anywhere

2.5 · Process gates — the checklist every future strategy passes before it ships

  1. Classify the view against the taxonomy (§2.2). The type fixes the evidence standard and the legal expression menu. A view that fits no row does not ship.
  2. Write the resolution spec first — metric, benchmark, date, data source — before any strategy work (this becomes the "Rules" object on the page, §3.3).
  3. Establish the consensus (Event/Relative): option-implied move/probability, PM price where liquid (source-tagged), or analyst median. No consensus → reclassify as shock or theme.
  4. Condition the analog sample on surprise sign; compute market-model ARs, CAAR, BMP + sign test (call the existing event_study.py — it is built and tested).
  5. Register every variant tried in a view-scoped trial group (record_and_deflate) — the screen width is the trial count; there is no such thing as an unregistered experiment.
  6. Build tiers from the conditioned evidence, not from the template ladder alone: direction from surprise gap, option moneyness from implied-vs-expected move, pair legs from cointegration, hedges from the transmission map's loser branch.
  7. Subtract costs, shrink, band (§2.4). If p50_net ≤ 0, the tier ships only as an honest no_edge teaching artifact or not at all.
  8. Grade with the five gates; write config.scores.edge_grade; the UI renders only from this block.
  9. One generator, one sync: a single script emits pack summaries + details from the same computation (kills the best_expression desync class permanently), stamping provenance (generated_at, engine version, universe hash).
  10. Adversarial pass before publish: one reviewer (human or agent) attempts to refute G1 and G5 in writing; the refutation attempt is stored with the view (this is the curation gate validate_for_review grown one level).
§3 · Part III

Experience redesign — prediction-market ease, quant honesty

3.1 · Borrow / reject — the design contract

What makes Kalshi and Polymarket effortless is not the betting — it is one dominant number per card, a plain-English question with a deadline, rules one scroll from the action, and complexity behind a toggle rather than removed. Pivot borrows the discipline and rejects the affordances:

3.3 · Detail page — the narrative spine

Risks & mitigations

  • Small-N honesty may read as weakness ("Unproven" everywhere). Mitigation: the grade chip's tap-through explains why and what would upgrade it — rigor as brand, the Kalshi lesson. A pack where everything honestly grades C still beats one fabricated A.
  • PM data availability in India (geo/liquidity). Mitigation: the frame is source-agnostic — option-implied moves and analyst consensus fill where PM prices are thin; the slot renders only with a real source.
  • Pinned tests encode today's verdict ceilings and trust-block shapes — the Edge Grade is deliberately layered on top of them (same ceilings, new combiner), not through them.
  • Scope creep into user-authored views — out of V1 per the spec; nothing here requires it.

Appendix — citations & formulas

Core references

  • MacKinlay (1997), "Event Studies in Economics and Finance," JEL 35(1) — market-model AR/CAR/CAAR, estimation vs event windows, small-N inference.
  • Savor & Wilson (2013), JFQA — 11.4bp announcement-day vs 1.1bp other days; >60% of the equity premium on 13% of days.
  • Lucca & Moench (2015), JF / FRBNY SR512 — ~49bp pre-FOMC drift, Sharpe 1.14; decayed post-2011 (the canonical published-anomaly decay).
  • Kuttner (2001), JME; Bernanke & Kuttner (2005), JF — surprise decomposition; expected component ≈ 0 price impact.
  • Bernard & Thomas (1989/90) — PEAD: top-minus-bottom SUE decile ≈ 4.2% over 60 days, positive 41 of 48 quarters; spread ≤3% by late 2010s.
  • Bailey & López de Prado (2012, 2014) — PSR, MinTRL, Deflated Sharpe: E[max SR_N] ≈ √V[SR]·[(1−γ)Φ⁻¹(1−1/N)+γΦ⁻¹(1−1/Ne)].
  • Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2017), JCF — PBO via CSCV; PBO > 0.5 = selection carries no information.
  • Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016), RFS — multiple-testing hurdle t > 3.0.
  • McLean & Pontiff (2016), JF — 97 predictors: −26% out-of-sample, −58% post-publication → the 50% shrinkage base rate.
  • Dubinsky, Johannes, Kaeck & Seeger (2019), RFS — event variance premium / IV crush (30–50% overnight); implied > realized ~70–75% of events; earnings-straddle hold-through ≈ 45.3% win, −3.2% mean (Volatility Box, 2021–25, N≈4,200).
  • Kelly (1956); MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba (2010) — half-Kelly ≈ 75% of growth at half the variance; estimated edges → fractional Kelly.
  • PM calibration: Polymarket Brier ≈ 0.084–0.09; Kalshi ≈ 0.05–0.06 at 200 days out; Clinton & Huang (2026, Vanderbilt) caution on thin markets → volume-weight the prior, shrink user probability w ≈ 0.3–0.5.
  • Costs (post-Oct-2024): delivery STT 0.1%/side; options STT 0.1% of premium (sell) + ~₹20/order + NSE txn ~0.03% + GST + stamp; single-stock option spreads often 1–5% of premium.

The three formulas the product displays (nothing else is shown as a forward number)

shrinkage      w    = N / (N + 30)
projection     p50  = w · median(AR_occ) − costs_rt        (+ band [w·P25 − c, w·P75 − c], tail = MC p05)
edge vs market gap  = (w_u·p_user + (1−w_u)·q) − q         (w_u ≈ 0.3–0.5; actionable only if |gap| > calibration error)
