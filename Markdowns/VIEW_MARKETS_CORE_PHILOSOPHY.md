# Thinking Framework for View Markets

## Context for Claude Code

Do not think of View Markets as:

* Themes
* Research reports
* News feeds
* Stock recommendations
* Prediction markets

Think of it as:

> A system that converts human opinions about the world into investable expressions.

The product starts with something fundamental:

**People naturally have opinions.**

People do not naturally have strategies.

Nobody wakes up thinking:

* Bull Call Spread
* Long Calendar Spread
* Pair Trade
* Long Duration Basket

People wake up thinking:

* Will Bitcoin keep going up?
* Will India become the next China?
* Is AI overhyped?
* Will defence stocks continue rallying?
* Will the government cut taxes?
* Will my team win tonight?
* Will oil cross $100?

The job of View Markets is to bridge:

Opinion
↓
Market View
↓
Investment Expression
↓
Strategy
↓
Deployment

---

# Why Questions Are Better Than Statements

Statements are passive.

Examples:

AI Adoption Theme
India Manufacturing Theme
Gold Theme

These are not engaging.

Questions force participation.

Examples:

Will AI create another tech boom over the next five years?
Will India become a manufacturing powerhouse by 2030?
Will Gold outperform equities this year?
Will defence stocks continue rallying?

Questions create:

1. Curiosity
2. Commitment
3. Opinion formation
4. Emotional involvement
5. Discussion and disagreement

The moment someone answers:

YES

they become psychologically invested.

Questions therefore become the fundamental primitive of View Markets.

The product should feel like:

"What do you believe?"

rather than:

"Read this market research."

---

# Questions Must Be Specific

A belief without:

* measurable outcome
* benchmark
* timeline

cannot resolve.

Bad:

AI will grow.
India will grow.
Manufacturing will boom.

These are narratives.

They are difficult to prove right or wrong.

Good:

Will Indian IT outperform Nifty over the next 12 months?
Will India GDP growth exceed consensus estimates over the next year?
Will AI beneficiaries outperform the market over the next three years?

Questions should preferably have:

* measurable outcome
* specific timeframe
* clear benchmark
* observable result

Specificity makes views actionable.

---

# View Categories

Not all questions are the same.

The system should reason about views in categories.

---

# Category 1: Event Views

Examples:

Will RBI cut rates next month?
Will OPEC cut production?
Will TCS beat earnings expectations?
Will the US attack Iran?

Properties:

* clear event
* resolution date
* measurable outcome
* binary answer

Structure:

Question
↓
Event
↓
Resolution
↓
Possible Expressions

These are closest to prediction markets and often generate the highest engagement.

---

# Category 2: Relative Views

Examples:

Will Gold outperform equities this year?
Will IT outperform Banks over six months?
Will India outperform China over the next decade?

Properties:

* measurable
* benchmark-based
* time-bound
* not purely binary economically

Structure:

Asset A
vs
Asset B
over
Time T

These are highly tradeable.

---

# Category 3: Price Target Views

Examples:

Will Bitcoin cross $100k this year?
Will crude oil hit $120?
Will Nifty reach 25,000 by year-end?

Properties:

* clear numeric threshold
* time-bound
* highly intuitive
* easy to understand

Structure:

Asset
→
Price Level
→
Time

These are extremely engaging and directly map to options and directional trades.

---

# Category 4: Range / Volatility Views

Examples:

Will Nifty stay within a 5% range this month?
Will Bitcoin remain above $50k for the next quarter?
Will volatility spike in the next 30 days?

Properties:

* focuses on movement rather than direction
* useful for options strategies
* measurable via ranges or volatility metrics

Structure:

Asset
→
Range / Volatility Condition
→
Time

These are ideal for advanced strategies like straddles, strangles, and volatility trades.

---

# Category 5: Sports & Real-World Outcome Views

Examples:

Will India win the World Cup?
Will Manchester City win the league?
Will a specific player score more than 30 goals this season?

Properties:

* extremely high engagement
* emotionally driven
* clear resolution
* not always directly tradeable

Structure:

Event
→
Outcome
→
Resolution

These views expand the system beyond finance and can later connect to indirect market expressions (e.g., sponsorships, betting proxies, sentiment-driven trades).

---

# Category 6: Themes

Examples:

Will AI create another technology boom?
Will India become a manufacturing powerhouse?
Will defence spending create a long-term supercycle?

Properties:

* narrative driven
* long duration
* difficult to resolve objectively
* often better expressed via baskets and themes

These views should generally be treated as long-term investment theses.

---

# Why Binary Questions Work

Binary questions are engaging.

Humans enjoy:

YES
NO

This makes participation easy.

It lowers cognitive effort.

The user does not need to understand:

* economics
* derivatives
* finance terminology

The user only needs an opinion.

This dramatically increases accessibility.

---

# Important Principle

Binary questions do NOT imply binary trades.

This distinction is critical.

Question:

Will Gold outperform equities?

YES:

Long Gold
Short Equities

NO:

Long Equities
Short Gold

Both sides have investable expressions.

Question:

Will US attack Iran?

YES:

Long Oil
Long Gold
Long Defence

NO:

No obvious trade exists.

No attack does not imply:

* Oil falls
* Gold falls
* Equities rise

Many other outcomes may occur.

Therefore:

The opposite of an event is not necessarily an inverse trade.

---

# Asymmetric Views

Many real-world events are asymmetric.

Question:

Will RBI cut rates?

YES:

Long Banks
Long Duration Assets

NO:

Could mean:

* Pause
* Hike
* Dovish pause
* Hawkish pause

These states have different market implications.

There may not be one clean trade.

Question:

Will OPEC cut production?

YES:

Long Energy

NO:

No direct expression may exist.

Question:

Will US attack Iran?

YES:

Strong expressions exist.

NO:

No high-conviction direct expression exists.

This is acceptable.

The system should never force a trade.

---

# Principle

Every question should have:

YES
NO

But not every answer needs an investment expression.

Possible outcomes:

YES Trade
NO Trade

YES Trade
NO Avoid

YES Trade
NO Alternative Scenarios

The absence of a trade is sometimes the correct answer.

This should be treated as a feature rather than a limitation.

---

# Thinking Framework For Any New View

Step 1

Is this question interesting?

Would ordinary people naturally have opinions about it?

---

Step 2

Can it be measured?

What determines whether it happened?

---

Step 3

Does it have a timeframe?

When does it resolve?

---

Step 4

Does it have market consequences?

Who wins?
Who loses?

---

Step 5

Are there investable expressions?

Can we build:

* baskets
* sector strategies
* options strategies
* pair trades
* hedges

---

Step 6

Are the effects symmetric?

Can both YES and NO generate trades?

Or only one side?

---

Step 7

How engaging is it?

Consider:

Curiosity
×
Debatability
×
Tradeability
×
Resolution Clarity

The best views score highly on all four dimensions.

# Desired Mental Model

Do not think:

Question
↓
Buy Stock

Think:

Question
↓
Take Stance
↓
Understand Consequences
↓
Find Expressions
↓
Deploy Strategy

The product is fundamentally:

Opinions about the world
↓
Investable expressions
↓
Deployable strategies

This philosophy should guide ideation and future View Markets design.
