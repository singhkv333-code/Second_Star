01 · What Pivot is today

The honest inventory — because the vision only matters if it composes from real parts.

Pivot is a chat-first investing copilot for Indian retail. One chat surface where a user asks in English or Hinglish and gets either a grounded answer (live Kite data, technicals, fundamentals, news, screens) or a built thing rendered as an editable card: a trading automation, an options strategy, a backtest, a paper trade, an IPO application. The execution model is register-not-execute — Pivot arms intent; the user fires in their own broker app.

ASK

Grounded market answers

Live prices, history + 20 indicators, fundamentals, screens, comparisons, correlation, news, events, after-tax yield math.

AUTOMATE

37-step-type workflow DSL

Price / indicator / compound / schedule / event / IPO / Polymarket triggers → orders, baskets, SL/TP, rebalance, cash sweep, drawdown protection.

F&O

Real options engine

Live chains with OI/IV/greeks, suggest–build–critique strategies, payoff diagrams, portfolio greeks, rolls.

BACKTEST

Rigor-grade simulation

Compound-DSL + pairs/portfolio engines, real Indian cost stack (~35–40 bps round trip), walk-forward, permutation.

PAPER

Simulated broker

Registered ideas fill at live prices into a paper book with NAV, P&L, tax summary, greeks.

SEED

Synthetic products (already!)

get_active_products + SyntheticSecurityCard already track maturity-dated products (SafeGrow, EarnMore, StormShield) — a primitive ancestor of custom securities.

The load-bearing observation

Custom Securities is usually pitched as a new product line. For Pivot it is a composition layer: option pricing & greeks, a lifecycle automation DSL with rebalance/roll/drawdown nodes, a cost-aware backtester, a paper book with NAV tracking, and register-not-execute compliance rails already exist and are live. Roughly 80% of the hard engineering for this feature has already shipped under other names.

02 · The thesis, restated from first principles

What a "custom security" actually is — and the answer to "why wouldn't they just buy the option?"

Strip the branding and a security is three things:

1

A payoff function

The mapping from "what the market does" to "what you get". Stocks are linear. Options are kinked. Structured notes are sculpted.

2

A manufacturing recipe

The basket of real instruments whose combined behaviour produces that payoff. Banks hide this. Replication exposes it.

3

A lifecycle policy

What happens between birth and maturity: rolls, rebalances, breaches, early exit. This is the part everyone forgets — and the part banks actually charge 2–3% p.a. for.

Retail investors can only buy pre-packaged payoffs. The Custom Securities idea is that Pivot becomes the compiler: the user states an outcome in scenario language ("I want Bitcoin-style upside but can't lose more than ₹20,000"), and Pivot solves for the recipe using live data, shows the exact payoff in a scenario table, and attaches the lifecycle automation. Users stop choosing securities and start choosing outcomes — the screenshot's three-question flow (max loss → theme → horizon) is the right intake, and "the product is the shape of the payoff curve" is the right endgame.

Why this beats "just buy the option" — the four real unlocks

DimensionStandalone instrumentComposed security
Downside shapeA naked call risks 100% of premium capital; a stock risks everything, alwaysDecouple upside from downside: risk only the interest of the capital, keep the principal floored
View expressibleDirectional only — "X up" or "X down"Relative ("INFY beats IT index"), macro-synthetic ("rupee depreciation" via gold + IT), event-windowed
Time controlStock = infinite exposure; option = one fixed windowEquity exposure for 3 years, insurance for only the 6 risky months — risk engineered across time
Crisis correlationA basket just averages; the hedge leg idlesRule-driven rebalancing harvests negative correlation — sell the winner, buy the loser, repeatedly

And two unlocks the earlier conversations under-weighted: no issuer credit risk (a bank note is an unsecured IOU; the replicated version is exchange-traded instruments in the user's own demat — when Credit Suisse-style events happen, that difference is everything), and the lifecycle is the product — a one-time basket decays; a security with attached roll/rebalance/breach automations stays true to its promised payoff. Pivot's workflow engine is precisely a lifecycle machine.

03 · Reality checks — what the idea is missing

You asked to be told what you're missing. These five things, in descending order of severity.

① The lot-size wall: the ₹1,00,000 example cannot be built

The classic Capital-Guaranteed Note example allocates ₹6,542 to a Nifty ATM call. But index options trade in lots — Nifty's lot is 65 (revised from 75, Oct 2025), and SEBI mandates index derivative contract value stay in the ₹15–20 lakh band. One 12-month ATM Nifty call ≈ 7–8% of notional ≈ ₹1.2–1.4 lakh of premium — eighteen times the example's budget.

Run the solver backwards: with the safety leg at ~7% (arbitrage fund), the option budget is 1 − 1/1.07 ≈ 6.5% of capital. Minimum capital for one lot = ₹1.27L ÷ 0.065 ≈ ₹19–20 lakh for the full 100%-protected, 100%-participation note. The fix isn't to abandon the idea — it's to tier it: (a) paper mode has no lot constraint — launch there; (b) partial protection drops the ticket fast (a 90% floor frees ~16% of capital for options → min ticket ≈ ₹8L); (c) ETF-only structures (barbell, glidepath, momentum-switch) work from ₹10,000; (d) stock options on cheaper underlyings shrink lots further. The product ladder must be honest about which payoffs exist at which ticket sizes.

② Protection is a maturity promise — mid-life the package will be red

"Capital protected" means protected at maturity. Six months in, the option leg may be down 60% on theta + a market drop, and the mark-to-market of the whole package reads −4%. A retail user who bought "protected" will panic-exit at precisely the wrong time, crystallising a loss the structure would have repaired. Every LogicCard therefore needs a mid-life value band ("between now and maturity this can read as low as −X% — that is normal and does not breach the floor") and the paper book must mark the security as one unit, not two confusing legs. This is a UX problem with the same importance as the solver.

③ The legs are taxed differently — the after-tax payoff is not the gross payoff

Arbitrage funds are taxed as equity (12.5% LTCG beyond ₹1.25L after 12 months) — which is exactly why they beat FDs as the safety leg for anyone above the 20% slab. But index option gains are non-speculative business income taxed at slab, and barbell rebalancing trades generate STCG events each trigger. A 30%-slab user's "+₹15,000 option win" is +₹10,500 after tax. The scenario table must be shown after tax and after the ~35–40 bps cost stack Pivot already models — this honesty is also the moat, because no bank term-sheet shows it.

④ Long-dated NSE option liquidity is thin

The structures want 12-month options; NSE liquidity lives in near-month contracts (quarterly/half-yearly exist but trade wide). Practical consequence: the engine must support roll-based replication — hold 1–3 month options and roll at expiry (Pivot already has trigger.expiry_day and roll_option_position). Rolling changes the economics (path dependency, roll cost each cycle) and the backtester must price that honestly before any real-money tier ships.

⑤ The regulatory line: a solver that maps risk preference → portfolio is close to "investment advice"

Three rails keep this buildable. First, replication-not-issuance: Pivot never pools money or issues paper — pooling would make it a collective investment scheme/AIF. The user owns each exchange-traded leg in their own demat. Second, register-not-execute (already Pivot's model): the user confirms every leg in their own broker app, aligned with SEBI's retail-algo posture. Third, the smallcase precedent: curated model baskets distributed via brokers, created by SEBI-registered RAs/RIAs, executed in the user's own account, is an accepted structure in India. Pivot's near-term framing must be user-directed templates — the user picks the template and parameters, Pivot computes the math (a calculator, not an adviser). The marketplace/curation phase later likely needs an RA registration or RA/RIA partners publishing on Pivot, smallcase-style. Plan the entity work in parallel with the build.

05 · The Outcome Compiler — how it's structured

Five layers. Four already exist in some form in the codebase.

1 · INTENTScenario-language intake (chat or 4-question flow)
→
2 · SOLVERLeg sizing against live yields, chains, lots
→
3 · PROOFScenario table + payoff curve + backtest, after tax & costs
→
4 · REGISTERSecurityCard → legs registered, user fires in broker
→
5 · LIFECYCLEWorkflow DSL: rolls, rebalances, breach alerts, maturity

Layer 1 — Intent new, thin

Formalise the screenshot's flow into four questions, then let chat fill them conversationally (most users will just type "protect my capital but give me Nifty upside for 2 years" and skip the form):

#QuestionMaps to
Q1Maximum loss you can tolerate? (0% / 5% / 10% / 25% / "I'm fine with full risk")floor_pct — the protection level; the single biggest driver of structure & min ticket
Q2What do you want exposure to? (Nifty / gold / a stock / a theme: AI, defence, pharma / Bitcoin-research-only)underlying[] — the growth leg(s)
Q3Time horizon? (6m / 1y / 2y / 5y)horizon — tenor, roll cadence, which safety instrument matures correctly
Q4 (the one the screenshot missed)Might you need this money early?liquidity_need — gates illiquid structures; drives the mid-life-band warning prominence

Layer 2 — Solver new core

A deterministic engine (not the LLM — the LLM parses intent; the solver does arithmetic) that takes a SecuritySpec and returns sized legs or an honest infeasibility with the nearest feasible alternative:

# SecuritySpec — the new first-class object (sibling of WorkflowSpec)
{
  "name": "My Nifty Seatbelt",            # user names it — ownership psychology
  "objective": { "floor_pct": 100, "participation": "max", "underlying": ["NIFTY"] },
  "horizon_months": 24,
  "capital_inr": 2000000,
  "mode": "paper",                          # paper | real
  "legs": [                                 # SOLVER OUTPUT, user-editable
    { "role": "floor",  "instrument": "ARBITRAGE_FUND", "alloc_inr": 1746000, "yield_pct": 7.0 },
    { "role": "growth", "instrument": "NIFTY 26000 CE", "lots": 1, "roll": "quarterly" }
  ],
  "lifecycle": {                            # compiles to existing workflow DSL nodes
    "roll_policy":      { "trigger": "expiry_day", "action": "roll_option_position" },
    "rebalance_policy": null,
    "breach_alerts":    [{ "if": "package_mtm < floor_projection - 3%", "notify": "push" }],
    "maturity":         { "action": "notify + propose unwind card" }
  }
}

Solver inputs Pivot already serves: arbitrage/liquid/G-Sec yields (compare_yields + mfapi.in for live fund NAVs), option chains with IV and greeks (Kite, get_option_chain), lot sizes (instrument master), the cost stack (trading_costs.py). Solver responsibilities: tenor-match the floor leg so it compounds to exactly the protected amount; maximise growth-leg participation subject to lot integrality; refuse infeasible specs loudly ("100% floor at ₹1L needs ~₹19L with real options — here's the same shape in paper mode, or a 90% floor at ₹8L, or the ETF barbell version at your size").

Layer 3 — Proof: the SecurityCard extends existing cards

The LogicCard concept, upgraded. The payoff diagram already exists in OptionStrategyCard; the equity-curve/backtest rendering already exists in FinancialBacktestCard. The SecurityCard composes them and adds the scenario table — after tax, after costs, with the mid-life band:

Scenario (Nifty, 24m)Floor legGrowth legPackage, grossPackage, after tax + costs

Illustrative @ ₹20L capital, 7% arbitrage yield, 1 Nifty lot (65), 30% slab on option P&L, equity LTCG on floor leg, ~35–40 bps round-trip costs. The solver computes these from live data per build — never hardcoded.

Plus on every card: a one-click historical backtest of the structure (the DSL/portfolio backtester already prices the rebalancing rules and costs) and the named-security register button. "My Bitcoin Seatbelt is up 12%" is a different emotional product than "my portfolio is up 4%" — naming is not cosmetic.

Layer 4 — Register & custody exists

Identical rail to today: legs become registered orders (/orders/register); in paper mode they fill into the paper book as one composite position with unit NAV (the IPO feature already proved paper credit-at-listing → NAV tracking); in real mode the user places each leg in their broker app and Pivot reconciles. The existing SyntheticSecurityCard/get_active_products machinery (maturity, yield, NAV, status) becomes the portfolio view for these.

Layer 5 — Lifecycle exists almost entirely

Every lifecycle policy compiles to workflow-DSL nodes that already ship: trigger.expiry_day + roll for the options leg; create_rebalancing_rule-style on-breach logic for the barbell (the 60/40 breach rule is literally the existing rebalance-on-breach trigger); trigger.compound on package MTM for breach alerts; trigger.schedule for maturity-day unwind proposal; notify.message throughout. The compiler emits a workflow; the scheduler runs it. This is why Pivot, specifically, can build this feature in weeks rather than quarters.

07 · Phased roadmap

Sequenced so every phase ships standalone user value and de-risks the next.

P0

Outcome Lab, paper-only · ~2–3 wks

SecuritySpec schema + solver, the 4-question intake wired into chat intent routing, SecurityCard (scenario table + payoff SVG + mid-life band), composite paper-book position with unit NAV, lifecycle compiled to existing DSL. No lot constraints, zero regulatory surface, full learning loop. Success metric: users build, name, and track securities; weekly retention on the NAV view.

P1

Real-money ETF tier · ~2 wks after P0

Barbell, Glidepath, Rupee-Play via register-not-execute (pure ETF baskets + rebalance workflows — no options, no lot walls, min ₹10k). Add the one-click structure backtest on every card. This needs MF/ETF leg support for the arbitrage/liquid floor — the same work unlocks feature #6 below.

P2

Options tier + roll engine · ~3–4 wks

Soft-Floor 90 and Capital-Guard for users at ₹8L+/₹19L+; quarterly roll automation hardened (expiry-day trigger → roll → reconcile); breach-alert workflows; honest roll-cost modelling in the backtest. Critique mode: paste any bank MLD term-sheet and Pivot shows the replicated version and the fee gap.

P3

Free-form compiler · ~4 wks

Beyond templates: "I want X exposure, max loss Y, horizon Z" for arbitrary underlyings → solver searches the template space + leg combinations and proposes 2–3 structures with trade-offs (the suggest→build→critique pattern the options engine already uses). Regulatory counsel engaged for the RA question before this goes loud.

P4

Marketplace · when P0 cohort proves retention

Publish anonymised securities with verified paper/real track records (smallcase precedent: RA/RIA creators, CA-verified performance, no backtest-as-performance claims); one-click replication re-solved at the copier's capital and current prices; creator subscriptions. GitHub-for-payoffs is the durable network effect — strategies, unlike content, compound.

08 · Endgame — custom risk curves as the product

The screenshot's closing intuition is right and worth stating as strategy: today people buy assets; on Pivot they specify max loss, expected return, theme exposure, horizon and the platform manufactures the combination. Stocks, options, bonds, ETFs, futures become ingredients; the product being sold is the shape of the payoff curve. Nobody serves this below SIF's ₹10L gate, and the $70B US buffer-ETF category proves the demand is mainstream, not exotic.

What makes it defensible for Pivot specifically: the compiler needs live chains + greeks + yields (built), a lifecycle automation engine (built), an honest cost/tax-aware simulator (built), a paper proving-ground (built), and a compliance rail regulators already tolerate (register-not-execute, built). Competitors would need to assemble all five before the marketplace network effect starts; Pivot needs to assemble a schema, a solver, and a card.

One sentence

Pivot: describe the outcome you want; we manufacture it from exchange-traded instruments you own — transparently, at retail size, with the lifecycle on autopilot.

09 · Eight more features worth building

Ranked by (user value × reuse of existing engines). Each line names what it reuses.

F1

Tax-Loss Harvesting Agent

get_tax_summary already finds harvest candidates. Turn it into a March-aware automation: scan holdings, propose loss-harvest + re-entry pairs respecting wash-timing, register the orders. India has no retail product doing this conversationally; it's seasonal, viral, and pure reuse.

F2

Portfolio X-Ray + Weekly Brief

Factor/sector concentration, correlation clusters (get_correlation_matrix), drawdown stress ("your book is 68% one factor"), delivered as a scheduled push digest. Turns Pivot from pull to push — retention infrastructure for everything else, including security NAV updates.

F3

Strategy Marketplace (workflows first)

The custom-securities marketplace (P4) generalised: any workflow/agent with a verified paper track record is publishable and copyable, re-parameterised to the copier's capital. Ship it for plain automations before securities to learn moderation + verification cheaply.

F4

Earnings-Season Copilot

get_upcoming_events + option chains + IV: "TCS reports Thursday — IV at 38 vs 24 norm, straddle prices a ±4.2% move; here's pre/post playbook + an IV-crush warning if you're long options." Event triggers and greeks already exist; this packages them into India's quarterly ritual.

F5

Regime Detector + Workflow Gating

A volatility/trend state machine (built on the indicator engine) exposing condition.regime in the DSL: "pause my dip-buyers in a downtrend regime." Also feeds Solver pricing and the brief. Quant depth no Indian retail product surfaces.

F6

Mutual-Fund / Direct-MF Legs

BSE StAR-style direct MF integration (or at minimum NAV-tracked registration). Required anyway for custom-security floor legs (arbitrage/liquid funds) — and standalone it unlocks SIP-in-MF, the largest retail behaviour in India, in chat.

F7

Counterfactual Time Machine

"What if I'd SIP'd ₹10k into NIFTYBEES since 2020 instead of holding FDs?" — backtester + cost model rendered as a shareable card. Cheap to build, emotionally powerful, an organic-acquisition loop (every share is an ad), and it teaches the scenario-thinking custom securities depend on.

F8

Hinglish Voice + Vernacular

Voice-first intake (the Sarvam integration points this direction already) for the next 100M-user cohort: speak the goal, get the card. Highest-leverage long-term distribution bet; everything above inherits it because chat is the single surface.

Sequencing note: F2 and F6 are force multipliers — F2 creates the daily/weekly touchpoint every other feature publishes into, and F6 is a hard dependency of the custom-securities floor leg. Build those two alongside P0/P1; the rest slot behind demand signals.
